\subsection{Design Approach}
The design of the smart trigger system follows a modular, iterative approach to integrate mechanical, electrical, and software components into the existing AR-15 firing control group. 

\textbf{Hardware and PCB Design:} We are using KiCad for the development of our custom Printed Circuit Board (PCB). This open-source suite allows for precise schematic capture and multi-layer PCB layout, which is necessary to meet the strict spatial constraints of the firearm's grip and receiver. For mechanical housing and the linear actuator interface, we utilize a 3D-modeling toolchain (AutoCAD, Inventor, or SolidWorks) to design parts for additive manufacturing. These tools allow us to verify tolerances before printing, ensuring the solenoid interacts correctly with the hammer and sear.

\textbf{Software Development:} The firmware for the system’s microcontroller is developed in VS Code, utilizing an interrupt-driven state machine to manage power consumption. We will use C/C++ or MicroPython for low-level hardware abstraction, ensuring low-latency communication between the RFID reader and the MCU.

\textbf{Measurement and Validation:} To ensure reliability, we use digital oscilloscopes and logic analyzers to verify signal integrity between the RFID module and the MCU. Mechanical components are validated using precision calipers to ensure the 3D-printed components fit within the standard AR-15 lower receiver without interfering with existing mechanical safeties.

\subsection{Current Status}
Our team has completed the foundational research and administrative setup necessary for the project’s execution. To date, the following milestones have been achieved:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Infrastructure and Version Control:} A shared Git repository has been established to manage firmware development and LaTeX documentation, ensuring seamless collaboration and version tracking.
    \item \textbf{Research and Review:} We have completed the initial Research Draft and a secondary analysis of existing market solutions, such as biometric and mechanical FSDs. These drafts underwent both peer and instructor review, leading to the solidification of our RFID-based approach.
    \item \textbf{Exploratory Prototyping:} We have conducted preliminary experimentation with RFID technology and STM32 microcontrollers during previous laboratory work. While we have not finalized the specific MCU or RFID chipset for the final build, this sandbox testing confirmed the feasibility of the proximity-based authentication required for the ring-tag interface.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Stretch Goals}
While the core project is defined by successful RFID-based blocking of the firing pin, we have identified several stretch goals that would enhance the system’s security and usability:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mobile Configuration App:} Developing a companion Android application (potentially via Kotlin) that allows users to manage authorized tags, view access logs, and check battery levels over a secure Bluetooth link.
    \item \textbf{Encrypted Tag Authentication:} Moving from basic UID reading to an encrypted handshake (such as AES-128 or Mifare DESFire) to prevent tag cloning or spoofing attacks.
    \item \textbf{Haptic Feedback:} Integrating a small vibration motor within the grip to provide the user with tactile confirmation when the system is successfully unlocked, reducing the cognitive load during high-stress scenarios.
    \item \textbf{Ultra-Low Power Deep Sleep:} Implementing an even more aggressive power management profile that uses an accelerometer to "wake" the system only when the firearm is moved, further extending battery life.
\end{itemize}
